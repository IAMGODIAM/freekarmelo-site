§02

How the System Fails

Step 1 — Transfer Initiated
TDCJ decides to move Karmelo to a new facility
No coordination with medical department. No verification that current medication orders will travel with the inmate.
Step 2 — Sending Facility
KOP medications left behind
TDCJ policy E-32.01 requires sending facility to transfer all issued KOP medications with current orders. In practice, this does not happen. Medication stays at the old facility or is discarded.
Step 3 — Transit
Karmelo is without medication during transport
Hours or days in transit with no anti-seizure medication. His brain is in a state of withdrawal. Risk of seizure increases with every hour.
Step 4 — Receiving Facility
24-hour screening misses the gap
TDCJ policy requires nursing staff to review health records within 24 hours of arrival. But if the sending facility didn't document the transfer, the receiving facility doesn't know what's missing.
Step 5 — Family Called
Parents get the call: "Bring his meds"
The family drives across Texas — sometimes hours — to deliver life-saving medication to a state prison. They should never have to do this. The state is legally obligated to provide it.
Step 6 — Repeat
Every. Single. Transfer.
This is not a one-time failure. It is a systemic, repeating failure that will continue for the duration of Karmelo's 35-year sentence unless forced to stop.
§03

The Law

U.S. Constitution — Eighth Amendment

Cruel and Unusual Punishment

"Deliberate indifference by prison personnel to a prisoner's serious illness or injury constitutes cruel and unusual punishment contravening the Eighth Amendment." — Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97 (1976)

TDCJ CMHC Policy E-32.01

Transfer & Continuity of Care — The Prison's Own Rule

Sending facility: "Inmates should be transferred with any issued KOP medications with a current order, medical equipment and supplies."

Receiving facility: Health record review by licensed nursing staff within 24 hours of arrival. Medications continued as ordered unless changed by unit provider.

The violation: TDCJ is failing its own policy. This is not a gap in the rules — it is a failure of execution.

Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)

Disability Discrimination

Epilepsy is a protected disability under the ADA. TDCJ must provide reasonable accommodation — including a reliable, uninterrupted medication delivery system that does not depend on family logistics. Requiring family to deliver medication is not a reasonable accommodation. It is the abdication of institutional responsibility.

42 U.S.C. § 1983 — Civil Rights Action

Liability for Non-Compliance

Any TDCJ official who knowingly fails to provide prescribed medication for a life-threatening condition is personally liable under §1983. The $2.25 million jury verdict in the LCS Texas prisoner death case (2014) demonstrates that courts will award heavily for medication failures in Texas prisons.
